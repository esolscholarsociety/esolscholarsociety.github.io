Who We Are

Built by a multilingual student,
for multilingual students.

Founded in August 2025 by Daniel Lee, the ESOL Scholar Society was born from personal experience: the challenge of navigating two languages, two cultures, and two worlds at once. We exist to make that journey easier for every student who comes after.

Member Stories

Real Journeys.
Real Voices.

Every officer in this club has lived the ESOL experience. These are their stories, in their own words.

🇰🇷 South Korea → Georgia, USA
Daniel Lee
President & Founder

In 2020, after living in Korea for years, I came back to the U.S. and had lost much of my English. Even though I should have entered 5th grade, I had to repeat 4th grade because my English level was too low.

I started school in Valdosta, Georgia, where there were very few Korean students. That made me feel extremely isolated. I remember sitting in class, wanting to raise my hand, but not knowing the words to ask a simple question. Sometimes I felt invisible. Other times I faced racism or teasing for not speaking English well.

Things changed after moving to Duluth and enrolling at Hull Middle School. I worked hard, and by 9th grade I reached 5.6 out of 6.0 on the ACCESS test. I know how painful it is to feel stuck because of language. That's why I want the ESOL Scholar Society to be a space where students don't just "survive" ESOL, but thrive.

🇹🇷 Turkey → Georgia, USA
Derin Bilsel
Vice President

I joined ESOL in 6th grade after moving to the U.S. from Turkey. Before that, I had studied in the U.S. for 3rd grade, where I was bullied for not knowing English well.

In middle school, my English improved, but not enough to exit ESOL until 7th grade. Being in ESOL felt limiting. I struggled with self-doubt, low grades, and fear of tests. To improve, I started with easy books like Diary of a Wimpy Kid, then pushed myself to read harder ones. For speaking, I started thinking in English. Eventually, I passed the ESOL exam and moved on to advanced classes. Today, I am in AP Precalculus with a 4.0 GPA. What truly motivated me was the belief my teachers and friends had in me, and now I want to provide that same encouragement to others.

🇻🇳 Vietnam → Georgia, USA
Brandon Pham
Social Media Manager

My name is Brandon, and when I came to America, I already had a foundation in English because I studied at an international school. But even with that, I realized quickly that grammar and basic communication skills weren't enough.

I struggled a lot with vocabulary, slang, and cultural expressions. Because of language issues, I had difficulty choosing classes I wanted, and in conversations I often felt left out. To overcome this, I dedicated time to reading books, watching movies, and learning new words daily. Over time, this consistent effort helped me improve my accent, vocabulary, and confidence. Now, I want to share that method with other ESOL students, to show them that even small, daily efforts can lead to big results.

🇻🇪 Venezuela → Georgia, USA
Carla Suarez Duran
Secretary

My name is Carla, and I'm from Venezuela. I moved to the U.S. almost two years ago. One of my biggest challenges wasn't just the language barrier. It was being too ashamed to ask for help. I'm very independent and always wanted to figure things out on my own, but that made me fall behind.

I remember one day, someone made fun of me while I was speaking English, right to my face, thinking I wouldn't understand. But slowly, things changed when I started forcing myself to talk, even if I wasn't sure it was correct. The more I talked, the more confident I became. Because I know how discouraging it feels to be ashamed of your English, I want to help others gain the courage to speak without fear.

🇲🇽 Mexico → Georgia, USA
Miley Rangel Aldana
Historian

My name is Miley, and as a child I spoke only Spanish while most of my peers spoke English. At first, I was not in ESOL classes, and that was very difficult. Teachers often underestimated my abilities and held me back compared to my classmates.

Eventually, I was placed into ESOL, and that changed everything. My ESOL teachers gave me the individual support I needed, and they believed in me. Today, I feel proud of how far I've come, and now I want to give back and help students who are still in ESOL so they don't have to feel as lost as I once did.

🇰🇷 South Korea → Georgia, USA
Yuyoung Kim
Community Outreach Coordinator

Two years ago I left Korea for Georgia, and everything felt unfamiliar. The new culture and language were a huge challenge. But instead of waiting for things to get easier, I chose to reach out first: to friends, to clubs, to anyone willing to connect.

I focused on what I could offer rather than what I lacked, and started teaching Korean language and culture in the community. That experience gave me real confidence, and my grades improved along with it. Now I want to help students who are still in that difficult early stage know that they are not alone, and that their story is just beginning.

Club Constitution

Our constitution establishes the foundation, structure, and values that guide everything we do.

I · Name & Purpose II · Membership III · Officers IV · Meetings V · Faculty Sponsor VI · Programs VII · Awards VIII · Finances IX · Amendments X · Ratification
Article I
Name & Purpose
Section 1 · Name

The name of this organization shall be the ESOL Scholar Society (hereinafter referred to as "the Society").

Section 2 · Mission Statement

The ESOL Scholar Society exists to empower multilingual students at Peachtree Ridge High School by providing academic support, building English communication confidence, and fostering a welcoming community where every student — regardless of language background — can thrive in and beyond the classroom.

Section 3 · Core Values
  • Speaking First — Confidence in verbal communication is the foundation of everything we do.
  • Inclusion — Every student deserves a space where their culture and language are respected.
  • Growth — We celebrate progress, not perfection.
  • Community — We support one another inside and outside of the classroom.
Article II
Membership
Section 1 · Eligibility

Membership in the ESOL Scholar Society is open to any student who meets one or more of the following criteria:

  • Currently enrolled in an ESOL course at Peachtree Ridge High School, OR
  • Has previously participated in the ESOL program at any school, OR
  • Is a multilingual student seeking additional English language support.
Section 2 · Non-Discrimination Policy

The Society shall not discriminate based on race, national origin, religion, gender, disability, or any other characteristic. All students who wish to learn and grow are welcome.

Section 3 · Active Membership Requirements

To be considered an active member in good standing, a member must:

  • Attend a minimum of 75% of scheduled weekly meetings per semester.
  • Participate in at least one unit presentation or activity per semester.
  • Demonstrate a commitment to personal growth and respect for fellow members.
Section 4 · Membership Dues

There are no mandatory dues required for general membership. The Society operates on a voluntary contribution basis for special events and materials only.

Article III
Officers & Leadership
Section 1 · Officer Positions

The Society shall be led by the following elected officers:

  • President — Daniel Lee
  • Vice President — Derin Bilsel
  • Secretary — Carla Suarez
  • Social Media Manager — Brandon Pham
  • Historian — Miley Rangel
Section 2 · Officer Duties

President: Presides over all meetings, sets the agenda, represents the Society to school administration and outside organizations, leads curriculum and program development.

Vice President: Supports the President, assumes presidential duties in the President's absence, coordinates member engagement and recruitment.

Secretary: Records and distributes meeting minutes, manages attendance records, handles official club correspondence.

Social Media Manager: Manages all club social media accounts, promotes meetings and events, documents club activities for public outreach.

Historian: Maintains a visual and written record of club activities, creates the end-of-year portfolio and scrapbook, documents member growth over time.

Section 3 · Officer Eligibility

To be eligible for an officer position, a candidate must:

  • Have been an active member of the Society for at least one full semester.
  • Maintain a passing GPA in all current courses.
  • Demonstrate leadership qualities and commitment to the Society's mission.
Section 4 · Elections

Officer elections shall be held annually in April for the following school year. Elections shall be conducted by secret ballot among active members. A simple majority (50% + 1) is required to elect any officer.

Section 5 · Removal from Office

An officer may be removed from their position for cause (including failure to perform duties, conduct unbecoming of the office, or attendance below requirements) by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of active members, following a written notice and opportunity to respond.

Article IV
Meetings
Section 1 · Regular Meetings

The Society shall meet weekly every Wednesday after school, for a duration of up to 90 minutes. Meetings shall follow the established curriculum units as outlined in the Society's Annual Program Plan.

Section 2 · Special Meetings

Special meetings may be called by the President or Faculty Sponsor with a minimum of 48 hours notice to all members.

Section 3 · Quorum

A quorum for conducting official club business shall consist of at least 50% of active members plus the presiding officer.

Section 4 · Meeting Format

Each meeting shall include: a brief check-in, a vocabulary or language warm-up activity, the main unit lesson or project, and a speaking practice component. All meetings shall conclude with a reflection or feedback moment.

Article V
Faculty Sponsor

The Society shall have a Faculty Sponsor who is a full-time employee of Peachtree Ridge High School. The Faculty Sponsor shall:

  • Attend and supervise all club meetings and events.
  • Serve as the official school liaison between the Society and administration.
  • Provide guidance and mentorship to club officers.
  • Co-sign any official club documents or funding requests.

The Faculty Sponsor is an advisory role and does not hold voting rights in club elections or decisions.

Article VI
Programs & Curriculum
Section 1 · Annual Curriculum

The Society shall operate on an eight-unit annual curriculum, with four units per semester. Each unit shall span approximately four weeks and focus on a progressive theme in English language development, with an emphasis on speaking confidence and real-world communication skills.

Section 2 · Unit Showcase

Each unit shall conclude with a member presentation or showcase activity, allowing members to demonstrate growth and practice public speaking in a supportive environment.

Section 3 · End-of-Year Showcase

The Society shall hold an annual End-of-Year Showcase open to school staff and, where appropriate, family members. This event shall celebrate member growth and highlight the work of the Society throughout the academic year.

Article VII
Awards & Recognition
Section 1 · Speaking MVP

At the conclusion of each semester, active members shall vote to recognize one member as the Semester Speaking MVP, awarded to the member who has demonstrated the greatest growth in verbal communication confidence.

Section 2 · Certificate of Participation

All active members who fulfill the attendance and participation requirements shall receive an official Certificate of Participation at the End-of-Year Showcase.

Section 3 · Graduation Cord (Future Goal)

The Society aspires to establish a graduation cord program pending school administration approval. Eligibility criteria for a graduation cord shall be established once the Society meets applicable school, state, or national recognition standards. Requirements under consideration include completion of two or more full academic years of active membership, demonstrated leadership or community service contribution, and successful completion of ESOL program benchmarks.

Article VIII
Finances

The Society shall not charge mandatory membership dues. Any fundraising activities must be approved by the Faculty Sponsor and school administration. All funds collected on behalf of the Society shall be managed in accordance with Peachtree Ridge High School and GCPS financial policies.

Article IX
Amendments

This Constitution may be amended by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of active members, provided that:

  • The proposed amendment has been submitted in writing to the Secretary at least two weeks prior to the vote.
  • All active members have been notified of the proposed amendment at least one week in advance.
  • The Faculty Sponsor has reviewed and approved the proposed amendment.
Article X
Ratification

This Constitution shall take effect upon approval by the club officers, Faculty Sponsor, and school administration.

Ratified by Daniel Lee, President · Derin Bilsel, Vice President · the Faculty Sponsor · and School Administration.
