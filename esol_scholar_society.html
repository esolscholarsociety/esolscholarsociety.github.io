Who We Are

Built by a multilingual student, for multilingual students.

Founded in August 2025 by Daniel Lee (이상윤), the ESOL Scholar Society was born from personal experience — the challenge of navigating two languages, two cultures, and two worlds at once. We exist to make that journey easier for every student who comes after.

Club Constitution

Our constitution establishes the foundation, structure, and values that guide everything we do.

Articles
I · Name & Purpose II · Membership III · Officers IV · Meetings V · Faculty Sponsor VI · Programs VII · Awards VIII · Finances IX · Amendments
Article IName & Purpose
Mission Statement
The ESOL Scholar Society exists to empower multilingual students at Peachtree Ridge High School by providing academic support, building English communication confidence, and fostering a welcoming community where every student — regardless of language background — can thrive.
Core Values
  • Speaking First — Confidence in verbal communication is the foundation of everything we do.
  • Inclusion — Every student deserves a space where their culture and language are respected.
  • Growth — We celebrate progress, not perfection.
  • Community — We support one another inside and outside of the classroom.
Article IIMembership
Eligibility
Open to any student currently enrolled in ESOL, who has previously participated in an ESOL program, or who is a multilingual student seeking additional English language support.
Active Membership Requirements
  • Attend a minimum of 75% of scheduled weekly meetings per semester.
  • Participate in at least one unit presentation or activity per semester.
  • Demonstrate commitment to personal growth and respect for fellow members.
Article IIIOfficers & Leadership
Current Officers (2025–2026)
  • President — Daniel Lee (이상윤)
  • Vice President — Derin Bilsel
  • Secretary — Carla Suarez Duran
  • Social Media Manager — Phạm Quang Trí Đức (Brandon Pham)
  • Historian — Miley Rangel Aldana
Elections
Held annually in April by secret ballot. A simple majority (50% + 1) is required to elect any officer.
Article IVMeetings
Every Wednesday after school for up to 90 minutes. Each meeting includes a vocabulary warm-up, main unit lesson, and speaking practice. Special meetings may be called with 48 hours notice.
Article VFaculty Sponsor
Ms. Erin Bridger — ELA Teacher & ESOL Department Lead, Peachtree Ridge High School. The Faculty Sponsor attends all meetings, serves as official school liaison, and provides guidance to club officers in an advisory role without voting rights.
Article VIPrograms & Curriculum
The Society operates on an eight-unit annual curriculum with four units per semester. Each unit spans approximately four weeks and concludes with a member showcase. An End-of-Year Showcase is held annually and is open to school staff and family members.
Article VIIAwards & Recognition
  • Semester Speaking MVP — Greatest growth in verbal communication confidence.
  • Certificate of Participation — All active members at End-of-Year Showcase.
  • Graduation Cord (Future Goal) — Pending school administration approval.
Article VIIIFinances
No mandatory membership dues. All fundraising activities must be approved by the Faculty Sponsor and school administration, managed per GCPS financial policies.
Article IXAmendments
Amended by two-thirds (2/3) vote of active members, with written proposal submitted two weeks prior, all members notified, and Faculty Sponsor approval obtained.

Member Stories

Real journeys.
Real voices.

Every officer in this club has lived the ESOL experience. These are their stories — in their own words.

Daniel Lee (이상윤)
President & Founder
🇰🇷 South Korea → Georgia, USA

In 2020, after living in Korea for years, I came back to the U.S. and had lost much of my English. Even though I should have entered 5th grade, I had to repeat 4th grade because my English level was too low.

I started school in Valdosta, Georgia, where there were very few Korean students. That made me feel extremely isolated. I remember sitting in class, wanting to raise my hand, but not knowing the words to ask a simple question. Sometimes I felt invisible — other times I faced racism or teasing for not speaking English well.

Things changed after moving to Duluth and enrolling at Hull Middle School. I worked hard, and by 9th grade I reached 5.6 out of 6.0 on the ACCESS test. I know how painful it is to feel stuck because of language. That's why I want the ESOL Scholar Society to be a space where students don't just "survive" ESOL — but thrive.

Derin Bilsel
Vice President
🇹🇷 Turkey → Georgia, USA

I joined ESOL in 6th grade after moving to the U.S. from Turkey. Before that, I had studied in the U.S. for 3rd grade, where I was bullied for not knowing English well.

In middle school, my English improved, but not enough to exit ESOL until 7th grade. Being in ESOL felt limiting — I struggled with self-doubt, low grades, and fear of tests.

To improve, I started with easy books like Diary of a Wimpy Kid, then pushed myself to read harder ones like Crime and Punishment and The Shining. For speaking, I started thinking in English. Eventually, I passed the ESOL exam and moved on to advanced classes. Today, I am in AP Precalculus with a 4.0 GPA. What truly motivated me was the belief my teachers and friends had in me — and now I want to provide that same encouragement to others.

Phạm Quang Trí Đức (Brandon Pham)
Social Media Manager
🇻🇳 Vietnam → Georgia, USA

My name is Brandon, and when I came to America, I already had a foundation in English because I studied at an international school. But even with that, I realized quickly that grammar and basic communication skills weren't enough.

I struggled a lot with vocabulary, slang, and cultural expressions. This made it hard to adjust — even though I thought I was prepared. Because of language issues, I had difficulty choosing classes I wanted, and in conversations I often felt left out. Many times, I had to rely on Google Translate to express myself, which was frustrating and sometimes embarrassing.

To overcome this, I dedicated time to reading books, watching movies, and learning new words daily. Over time, this consistent effort helped me improve my accent, vocabulary, and confidence. Now, I want to share that method with other ESOL students — to show them that even small, daily efforts can lead to big results.

Carla Suarez Duran
Secretary
🇻🇪 Venezuela → Georgia, USA

My name is Carla, and I'm from Venezuela. I moved to the U.S. almost two years ago. One of my biggest challenges wasn't just the language barrier — it was being too ashamed to ask for help. I'm very independent and always wanted to figure things out on my own, but that made me fall behind.

I remember one day, someone made fun of me while I was speaking English, right to my face, thinking I wouldn't understand. That moment stuck with me, and after that, I avoided speaking whenever possible.

But slowly, things changed when I started forcing myself to talk — even if I wasn't sure it was correct. I listened carefully to how people spoke and practiced by imitating them. The more I talked, the more confident I became. Now I'm out of ESOL, and all of my classes are in English. Because I know how discouraging it feels to be ashamed of your English, I want to help others gain the courage to speak without fear.

Miley Rangel Aldana
Historian
🇲🇽 Mexico → Georgia, USA

My name is Miley, and as a child I spoke only Spanish while most of my peers spoke English. At first, I was not in ESOL classes, and that was very difficult. Teachers often underestimated my abilities and held me back compared to my classmates.

Socially, it was even harder — I was already a naturally quiet kid, and the language barrier made it difficult to connect with others. I also faced racist comments from peers, which made me feel even more embarrassed about not knowing English well.

Eventually, I was placed into ESOL, and that changed everything. My ESOL teachers gave me the individual support I needed, and they believed in me. They worked patiently with me until I caught up. Today, I feel proud of how far I've come — and now I want to give back and help students who are still in ESOL so they don't have to feel as lost as I once did.
