Who We Are

Built by a multilingual student, for multilingual students.

Founded in August 2025 by Daniel Lee (이상윤), the ESOL Scholar Society was born from personal experience — the challenge of navigating two languages, two cultures, and two worlds at once. We exist to make that journey easier for every student who comes after.

Our Mission

More than English.
A sense of belonging.

We help multilingual students at Peachtree Ridge High School succeed academically, communicate confidently, and feel at home — no matter where they’re from.

📚

Academic Support

Weekly sessions focused on vocabulary, academic English, and the language skills needed to thrive in every classroom.

🎤

Speaking Confidence

Structured speaking practice, presentations, and real-world exercises that build confidence from the inside out.

🤝

Community

A safe, welcoming space where students from across the world can connect and support each other.

🌐

Cultural Celebration

Project-based learning that honors where our members come from, using culture as the bridge to language growth.

Member Stories

Real journeys.
Real voices.

Every officer in this club has lived the ESOL experience. These are their stories — in their own words.

🇰🇷 South Korea → Georgia, USA
Daniel Lee (이상윤)
President & Founder

In 2020, after living in Korea for years, I came back to the U.S. and had lost much of my English. Even though I should have entered 5th grade, I had to repeat 4th grade because my English level was too low.

I started school in Valdosta, Georgia, where there were very few Korean students. That made me feel extremely isolated. I remember sitting in class, wanting to raise my hand, but not knowing the words to ask a simple question. Sometimes I felt invisible — other times I faced racism or teasing for not speaking English well.

Things changed after moving to Duluth and enrolling at Hull Middle School. I worked hard, and by 9th grade I reached 5.6 out of 6.0 on the ACCESS test. I know how painful it is to feel stuck because of language. That’s why I want the ESOL Scholar Society to be a space where students don’t just “survive” ESOL — but thrive.

🇹🇷 Turkey → Georgia, USA
Derin Bilsel
Vice President

I joined ESOL in 6th grade after moving to the U.S. from Turkey. Before that, I had studied in the U.S. for 3rd grade, where I was bullied for not knowing English well.

In middle school, my English improved, but not enough to exit ESOL until 7th grade. Being in ESOL felt limiting — I struggled with self-doubt, low grades, and fear of tests. To improve, I started with easy books like Diary of a Wimpy Kid, then pushed myself to read harder ones. For speaking, I started thinking in English. Eventually, I passed the ESOL exam and moved on to advanced classes. Today, I am in AP Precalculus with a 4.0 GPA. What truly motivated me was the belief my teachers and friends had in me — and now I want to provide that same encouragement to others.

🇻🇳 Vietnam → Georgia, USA
Phạm Quang Trí Đức (Brandon Pham)
Social Media Manager

My name is Brandon, and when I came to America, I already had a foundation in English because I studied at an international school. But even with that, I realized quickly that grammar and basic communication skills weren’t enough.

I struggled a lot with vocabulary, slang, and cultural expressions. Because of language issues, I had difficulty choosing classes I wanted, and in conversations I often felt left out. To overcome this, I dedicated time to reading books, watching movies, and learning new words daily. Over time, this consistent effort helped me improve my accent, vocabulary, and confidence. Now, I want to share that method with other ESOL students — to show them that even small, daily efforts can lead to big results.

🇻🇪 Venezuela → Georgia, USA
Carla Suarez Duran
Secretary

My name is Carla, and I’m from Venezuela. I moved to the U.S. almost two years ago. One of my biggest challenges wasn’t just the language barrier — it was being too ashamed to ask for help. I’m very independent and always wanted to figure things out on my own, but that made me fall behind.

I remember one day, someone made fun of me while I was speaking English, right to my face, thinking I wouldn’t understand. But slowly, things changed when I started forcing myself to talk — even if I wasn’t sure it was correct. The more I talked, the more confident I became. Because I know how discouraging it feels to be ashamed of your English, I want to help others gain the courage to speak without fear.

🇲🇽 Mexico → Georgia, USA
Miley Rangel Aldana
Historian

My name is Miley, and as a child I spoke only Spanish while most of my peers spoke English. At first, I was not in ESOL classes, and that was very difficult. Teachers often underestimated my abilities and held me back compared to my classmates.

Eventually, I was placed into ESOL, and that changed everything. My ESOL teachers gave me the individual support I needed, and they believed in me. Today, I feel proud of how far I’ve come — and now I want to give back and help students who are still in ESOL so they don’t have to feel as lost as I once did.

🇰🇷 South Korea → Georgia, USA
Yuyoung Kim (김유영)
Community Outreach Coordinator

Two years ago I left Korea for Georgia, and everything felt unfamiliar. The new culture and language were a huge challenge. But instead of waiting for things to get easier, I chose to reach out first — to friends, to clubs, to anyone willing to connect.

I focused on what I could offer rather than what I lacked, and started teaching Korean language and culture in the community. That experience gave me real confidence, and my grades improved along with it. Now I want to help students who are still in that difficult early stage know that they are not alone — and that their story is just beginning.
